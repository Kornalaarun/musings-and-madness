## On Anger

> "Anybody can become angry-that is easy; but to be angry with the right person, and to the right degree, and at the right time, and for the right purpose, and in the right way-that is not within everybody's power and is not easy." - Aristotle

Anger stems from the fight part of the fight or flight response. It is a high arousal state that leaves you on edge. Anger is the most seductive of negative emotions because of the inner monologue that justifies venting rage.

The universal trigger for being angered is a sense of being endangered - physically or otherwise. Anger creates a background of action readiness which lasts for hours and sometimes days, keeping the emotional brain in readiness for arousal.

This hair trigger condition explains why people are more prone to anger if they have already been provoked or slightly irritated at something else. Anger builds on Anger. This high level of excitation fosters an illusion of power and invulnerability that may inspire and facilitate aggression.

**What Works**

One way of diffusing anger is to seize and challenge the thoughts that trigger surges of anger. Timing matters. The earlier in the anger cycle that you do this, the more effective it will be. This does not work if the people are highly enraged (cognitive in capacitance or simply put, inability to think straight).

Another way is cooling off, where you go to place that doesn't have triggers for rage and wait off the excitation period. Distraction is a high powerful mood altering device. The cooling off period must not be used to pursue the train of anger and thus must be an active engagement.

Relaxation techniques like breathing exercises move body to a low arousal state and help control anger (which is a high arousal state)

Exercise also helps for somewhat the same reason. After high levels of physiological activation during a workout, the body rebounds to low levels.

Self-awareness helps capture hostile thoughts as they arise. Once captured they can be appropriately challenged.

**What does not work**

Venting / Catharsis actually prolongs the angry state. It is sometimes helpful to confront but what works is taking time to cool off before you confront appropriately.

## On Worry

The reaction that underlies worry is the vigilance to potential danger that has been essential to survival over the course of evolution. When fear triggers the emotional brain, part of resulting anxiety fixates attention on the threat at hand, forcing mind to obsess about how to handle it and ignoring everything else.

Worry is a rehearsal of what might go wrong and how to handle it. It is about coming up with solutions by anticipating dangers before they arise.

The problem is when we get stuck in worry loop without getting anywhere close to a solution. The worry seems to come from nowhere, are uncontrollable and impervious to reason. It locks the worrier into a single impervious view of the worry and inhibits creative problem solving that can provide potential solutions.

Worry has a partial payoff which is highly reinforcing to the habit to a point that it sometimes becomes a mental addiction.

**What works**

Self awareness. Catching worrisome episodes as close to starting as possible. Identifying cues for anxiety, identifying situations and thoughts that trigger worry can help.

Practice relaxation methods. Just relaxation won't work you have to actively challenge the worrisome thought otherwise the worry spiral will keep coming back.

Mindfulness and healthy skepticism would help control worry. For disorders and phobias seek professional help. Combination of medication and therapy is needed.

**What does not work**

Advice like "stop worrying" or "don't worry be happy"

## On Melancholy

Sadness closes down our interests in diversion and pleasures, fixes attention on what has been lost and saps our energy for starting new endeavors - at least for the time being. In short it forces a kind of reflective retreat from Life's busy persuits, and leaves us in a suspended state to mourn loss, mull over its meaning and finally make psychological adjustments and new plans that will allow our lives to continue.

Bereavement is useful. Full blown depression is not.

Depression may need professional assistance but sub-clinical depression (or out of control melancholy) can be handled if people have the internal resources.

**What works**

Socializing with friends and family. Any activity that does not give you time to mull over things.

Challenge depressing thoughts.

Chose distractions wisely. Depression has a way of perpetuating itself in the kind of distractions we choose.

Aerobic workouts work for people who generally do not workout.

Cheering yourself up through treats and sensual pleasures

A more constructive approach to mood lifting is engineering small / easy wins - Completing a long delayed chore, clearing tasks

Lifts to self image are cheering - even in the form of dressing up and putting on makeup

Cognitive reframing : Seeing things in a different frame

Downward comparisons make you feel better

Helping others in need. Depression feeds on rumination and preoccupations with the self, helping others lifts us out of those preoccupations and pushes us to empathize.

Praying if you are very religious helps with all moods, specially depression

**What does not work**

Staying alone : Gives you more free time to ruminate. It adds a sense to loneliness and isolation to sadness.

Overeating comfort food, alcoholism, drugs

Alcohol is a CNS depressant and only adds to the feeling of depression
