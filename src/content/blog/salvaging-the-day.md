If something seems off the mark, my head uses it as an excuse to give up and let things go to hell. This is why if a day starts on a bad note, there is no attempt to salvage the rest of the day. If you break one habit, or fall into old patterns once, the entire day is already registered as a failure. If both 30% and 80% are treated as failure your day becomes either 100% or 0%. This is the perfectionist mentality that causes more bad than good.

That is not to say that you settle for subpar results. Going for and delivering extraordinary results is what leads to greatness and job satisfaction. So what can be done here?

I think a way to tackle this duality lies in how we define and measure success. Currently what counts as a win is too broad. There is a need to segregate work which are concrete, measurable and achieve able units and processes which are more iterative micro changes. Using the same metric for both is a myopic strategy. At work you should always aim to over perform. Processes need more patience. While building habits you will have relapses. You need to dust up and get back in the process. Any process which tries to radically change your day or how you work is doomed to fail. Micro changes over days and weeks are more effective and have compounding effect.

Building healthy habits is a process. Reading is a process. Learning new things is a process. Working out is a process. Your career is a process. Your life is a process. Make micro changes and be more forgiving.

---

- Reset after every regression.
- A day does not have to be perfect for you to do extraordinary work.
- Have checkpoints through the day to reset.
- Remember progress in processes is made incrementally. Avoid the allure of a momentous change. Be okay with small frequent changes.
